## Methodology

Each of the 12 concepts from Phase 1 was interrogated as a commercial PC property that must hold a player's attention for 15–20 hours, not just sound interesting in a pitch.

**Investigation questions applied to every concept:**
1. Can the central mechanic sustain 10–20 hours of enjoyable gameplay?
2. Can gameplay evolve meaningfully, or will it become repetitive?
3. Are there enough opportunities for interesting level design?
4. Can it support memorable characters, environments, encounters, and progression?
5. Does it offer exceptional Pixel Art environments and animation?
6. Would players genuinely want to purchase and play this?
7. Does it have a strong international identity / recognizability?
8. What are its biggest creative and production weaknesses?
9. What could make it exceptional rather than merely interesting?
10. What existing games should we study for the competitive landscape?

**Weighted scoring (out of 10 per criterion):**
| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---|
| Originality | 20% |
| Gameplay Potential | 20% |
| Creative Identity | 15% |
| Emotional Impact | 10% |
| World-Building Potential | 10% |
| Visual Potential | 10% |
| Commercial Appeal | 10% |
| Development Feasibility | 5% |

Every score below is justified, then converted to a weighted total out of 100.

### 1. Resonance Keeper

**Blind lighthouse keeper who "sees" only through sound echoes.**

| # | Investigation | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sustain 10–20h? | **At risk.** Echolocation is a strong hook, but the core loop (move → emit sound → interpret ripple → avoid) can exhaust players if every area feels the same. Tension cannot be maxed for 20 hours; it needs quieter, exploratory stretches. |
| 2 | Gameplay evolution? | **Moderate.** Can evolve via new sound sources (whistle, stomp, bell, ship horn, beacon), enemy hearing profiles, weather (rain masks sound), and surface types that absorb vs. reflect sound. Needs deliberate pacing: tutorial zone → cliff zone → shipwreck zone → fog-city zone. |
| 3 | Level design opportunities? | **Good but constrained.** Levels must be readable through sound alone, which limits visual complexity. Strong potential for "sound puzzles" (e.g., echo off a wall to reveal a hidden ledge; throw an object to distract a hunter). |
| 4 | Characters / progression? | **Limited cast.** Protagonist is isolated by design (a strength and weakness). Progression comes through new tools (louder horn, dampened boots) and beacon upgrades. Memorable characters are hard to fit into a lone-keeper fantasy. |
| 5 | Pixel Art potential? | **Exceptional.** Near-black canvas with crisp ripple expansions, limited palette blooms (amber beacon, indigo sea, pale foam), and particle decay. Highly screenshot-friendly in motion. |
| 6 | Would players buy it? | **Niche but real.** Horror/tension indies sell (Lethal Company, Phasmophobia), but the blind mechanic may frustrate casual players. Accessibility settings (subtle visual hints) are essential. |
| 7 | International identity? | **Very strong.** A dark screen pierced by sound ripples is unmistakable. Recognizable without a title. |
| 8 | Biggest weaknesses? | Player frustration/disorientation; narrow audience; difficulty communicating "how much sound is too much" without UI clutter. |
| 9 | What makes it exceptional? | World-class spatial audio design, gradual mechanic teaching, and moments where silence is the solution rather than the enemy. |
| 10 | Competitive landscape | Study: *Perception* (blind protagonist), *The Vale* (audio-only), *Lurking* (sound-based stealth), *Dark Echo* (sound-as-vision). Differentiate via lighthouse/beacon structure and coastal setting. |

**Scores:** Originality 9 / Gameplay 6 / Identity 9 / Emotional 7 / World 6 / Visual 9 / Commercial 5 / Feasibility 6  
**Weighted total:** (1.8 + 1.2 + 1.35 + 0.7 + 0.6 + 0.9 + 0.5 + 0.3) = **73.5 / 100**

### 2. Fading Ink

**Memory diver recovers objects from dying memories before they crumble.**

| # | Investigation | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sustain 10–20h? | **Moderate.** Decay is a compelling ticking clock, but "find object before it fades" risks sameness across 8–10 memory levels. Sustainability depends on each memory having distinct geometry, decay patterns, and emotional stakes. |
| 2 | Gameplay evolution? | **Good.** New memory types (childhood, trauma, celebration), objects with different decay rates, client hub interactions, and "frayed" vs. "stable" memory zones create variety. Late-game could let the player slow decay at a cost. |
| 3 | Level design opportunities? | **Strong.** Surreal, shifting spaces (tilted bedrooms, endless train stations) allow non-Euclidean or scripted geometry that would feel gimmicky elsewhere but fits memory logic. |
| 4 | Characters / progression? | **Strong emotional core.** Clients in the hub office carry narratives; memory fragments are characters from their past. Progression = recovered objects that change the client's present-day dialogue and office state. |
| 5 | Pixel Art potential? | **Excellent.** Soft pastels desaturating into glitch/static; clean line work dissolving into noise. Decay effects are visually distinctive and cheap to animate (sprite degradation). |
| 6 | Would players buy it? | **Yes, narrative-indie audience.** *Gris*, *Spiritfarer*, *Before Your Eyes* prove emotional pixel-art narratives sell. Decay mechanic is a fresh twist on the "dream level" trope. |
| 7 | International identity? | **Strong but familiar.** "Memory diving" echoes *Psychonauts* and *Beyond: Two Souls*; the decay twist is the differentiator, not the premise itself. |
| 8 | Biggest weaknesses? | Save-state complexity (objects recovered persist across decaying levels); risk of frustration if decay is too fast; narrative-heavy games live or die by writing quality. |
| 9 | What makes it exceptional? | If client stories are genuinely moving and decay forces hard choices (save this object or that one?), the mechanic becomes emotional, not just a timer. |
| 10 | Competitive landscape | Study: *Psychonauts*, *Before Your Eyes*, *Somerville*, *Read Only Memories*, *Fading Afternoon*. Differentiate via real-time decay as a literal mechanical force, not just an aesthetic. |

**Scores:** Originality 7 / Gameplay 7 / Identity 7 / Emotional 9 / World 7 / Visual 8 / Commercial 7 / Feasibility 5  
**Weighted total:** (1.4 + 1.4 + 1.05 + 0.9 + 0.7 + 0.8 + 0.7 + 0.25) = **72.0 / 100**

| 9 | What makes it exceptional? | If letters tell interwoven stories across islands and the player genuinely cares about the recipients, the terraforming becomes emotional rather than mechanical. |
| 10 | Competitive landscape | Study: *Animal Crossing*, *Stardew Valley*, *Windbound*, *Strange Horticulture* (letter reading), *A Short Hike*. Differentiate via payload-as-world-change rather than payload-as-currency. |

### 4. Chronoflora

**Gardener tends one plot across centuries; seeds planted in one era bloom in another.**

| # | Investigation | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sustain 10–20h? | **Strong.** Gardening loops are proven for 20+ hours (*Stardew*, *A Little to the Left*). Cross-era causality gives every planting long-term weight, so actions feel consequential, not repetitive. |
| 2 | Gameplay evolution? | **Strong.** New eras (prehistory, antiquity, industrial, far-future), era-specific tools/pests/climate, hybrid plants with temporal effects, and unlockable plot expansions create clear progression arcs. |
| 3 | Level design opportunities? | **Good.** One location across four eras means spatial mastery is rewarded; the player learns the plot and watches it transform. Era transitions at fixed camera points allow precise, hand-crafted environmental storytelling. |
| 4 | Characters / progression? | **Moderate cast.** Few direct characters; story emerges through era-specific flora, ruins left by past civilizations, and the garden's own history. Progression = new seeds, tools, era access, and hybrid discoveries. |
| 5 | Pixel Art potential? | **Exceptional.** Four distinct era palettes (ochre prehistory, teal industrial, bioluminescent future) at the same camera position; detailed flora; smooth cross-era transitions. Highly recognizable in still frames. |
| 6 | Would players buy it? | **Yes.** Cozy gardening audience is large and underserved on PC; the time-travel twist is easily communicated in a trailer (seed → centuries → tree). |
| 7 | International identity? | **Strong.** A single garden aging across epochs is a distinctive visual and mechanical signature. |
| 8 | Biggest weaknesses? | Single plot could feel claustrophobic if era maps are too similar; gardening saturation means the cross-era hook must be front-and-center in marketing; plant state persistence across eras requires disciplined data design. |
| 9 | What makes it exceptional? | If cross-pollinated hybrids create dramatic, surprising world changes (a flower in antiquity becomes a bridge in the future), the player feels like a gardener of history. |
| 10 | Competitive landscape | Study: *Stardew Valley*, *A Little to the Left*, *Terra Nil*, *Botany Manor*, *Animal Crossing*. Differentiate via time as the core resource, not just a setting. |

### Long-Term Gameplay Potential (15–20 hours)

**Winner: Chronoflora**

Gardening is one of the few loops proven to sustain 20+ hours without fatigue (*Stardew Valley*, *Animal Crossing*). Cross-era causality means every player action has delayed, visible consequences, which creates intrinsic motivation to return. Resonance Keeper's tension loop is brilliant but harder to sustain at high intensity for 20 hours without burnout. Fading Ink's decay loop is emotionally compelling but mechanically thinner; its longevity depends entirely on narrative quality rather than systemic depth.

### Commercial Potential

**Winner: Chronoflora (narrowly over Airsong Postal)**

Among the top three, Chronoflora has the strongest commercial profile: the cozy/gardening audience is large and active on PC, and the time-travel twist is instantly communicable in a 30-second trailer (plant a seed → jump centuries → see a tree). Resonance Keeper is the weakest commercially (niche horror, accessibility challenges, dark screenshots that don't read well in small thumbnails). Fading Ink sits in the middle — narrative indies sell well, but decay-as-frustration could limit word-of-mouth.

### Artistic and Creative Identity

**Winner: Resonance Keeper**

No other concept matches Resonance Keeper's immediate, unmistakable signature: a dark screen pierced by expanding sound ripples, amber beacon light, indigo sea. It is recognizable without a title.
